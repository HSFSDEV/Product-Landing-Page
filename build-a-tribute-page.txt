      <h1 id="title">Marie Curie</h1>
      <p>Marie Curie, orig. Maria Skłodowska, (born Nov. 7, 1867, Warsaw, Pol., Russian Empire—died July 4, 1934, near Sallanches, France), Polish-born French physical chemist.</p>
      <div id="img-div"><img id="image" src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c8/Marie_Curie_c._1920s.jpg/500px-Marie_Curie_c._1920s.jpg">
        <div id="img-caption">Pioneer of Radioactive Research</div>
        <div id="tribute-info">
          <h2 id="date">1883-85</h2>
          <ul>
            <li>Skłodowska wins a gold medal as top student in her class upon completion of her secondary education.</li></ul>
          <h2 id="date">1891-94</h2>
          <ul>
            <li>Skłodowska starts going by the name Marie when she moved to Paris to study at Sorbonne in 1891.
          </li>
            <li>In the spring of 1894, she meets Pierre Curie while he was completing his doctorate of science.
          </li></ul>
          <h2 id="date">1895</h2>
          <ul>
            <li>Marie and Pierre marry on July 25. This began a partnership that would soon achieve results of historical significance.
            </li></ul>
          <h2 id="date">1896-98</h2>
          <ul>
            <li>In 1896 a French scientist named Henri Becquerel finds that the element uranium gives off unusual rays of energy. He passes his findings on to Marie, who begins studying the phenomenon, which she later names radioactivity.</li>
            <li>In 1898 the Curies announce their discovery of two new elements, radium and polonium. They name polonium after Marie’s homeland of Poland.</li></ul>
          <h2 id="date">1903</h2>
          <ul>
            <li>Marie earns her doctorate of science in June, becoming the first woman in France to receive a doctoral degree.</li>
            <li>In November Marie and Pierre share with Becquerel the Nobel Prize for Physics for the discovery and research of radioactivity. Marie is the first woman to receive the honor.</li></ul>
            <h2 id="date">1906</h2>
            <ul>
              <li>Pierre dies in a traffic accident on April 19. Marie takes over his professorship at the Sorbonne in May. She is the first woman to teach there. She devotes all of her energy to completing alone the scientific work that she and Pierre had undertaken.</li></ul>
              <h2 id="date">1910-11</h2>
              <ul>
                <li>Marie’s fundamental treatise on radioactivity is published.</li>
                <li>Marie is awarded the Nobel Prize for Chemistry, for the isolation of pure radium.</li></ul>
              <h2 id="date">1914–18</h2>
              <ul>
                <li>World War I begins in 1914. Throughout the war Marie, with the help of her daughter Irène devotes herself to making X-ray technology available for use in the medical treatment of wounded soldiers.</li></ul>
              <h2>1922</h2>
              <ul>
                <li>From this date Marie focuses her research on the chemistry of radioactive substances and the medical applications of these substances.</li></ul>
              <h2 id="date">1932</h2>
              <ul>
                <li>Marie helps open the Radium Institute in Warsaw. Her sister, Bronislawa, is named director of the institute.</li></ul>
              <h2 id="date">July 4, 1934</h2>
              <ul>
                <li>Marie dies near Sallanches, France. Her death is the result of leukemia caused by exposure to radiation.</li></ul>
              <h2 id="date">1944</h2>
              <ul>
                <li>American chemists discover a new element. They name it curium in honor of Marie and Pierre Curie.</li></ul>
          <blockquote>"Nothing in life is to be feared; it is only to be understood"<cite> -Marie Curie</cite></blockquote>
